# Content quality and E-E-A-T

Start with the page's promise and the question the intended reader brings to it. Map headings, primary answers, support for claims, source links, internal navigation and next actions. Compare important pages for repeated text or missing details. Use word counts or readability measurements only if the underlying text and method are available.

Apply [E-E-A-T criteria](../topics/eeat-framework.md) to observable evidence of experience, expertise, authority, and trust. Give more weight to accuracy and support for consequential claims. Explore the source of any contradictory date or claim. A missing byline is not decisive by itself. Flag generic or unsupported content without speculating about whether it was AI-generated.

Give specific changes tied to the audience, intent, and observed page. Separate source-backed recommendations from proposed additions requiring research.
